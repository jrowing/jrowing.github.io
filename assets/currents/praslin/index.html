How this model works, and how far to trust it

It's a physics model that hasn't yet been checked against measured currents. The tides are calibrated to the Mahé tide gauge, but no current measurements around Praslin went into it. Use it for the pattern and timing of the flow, and treat the speeds as plausible to within about a factor of two. Never use it as the only basis for a safety decision.

What's in it

  • Tides. A 2D depth-averaged shallow-water model (nonlinear, quadratic bottom friction, Coriolis) covering the whole Seychelles Plateau at 1.85 km resolution. Its boundary tide is calibrated so the model reproduces the five main constituents at the Pointe La Rue gauge (UHSLC station 121, hourly data for 2022–24). A nested 150 m model around Praslin, Curieuse, La Digue, Aride and the Sisters is driven by the plateau model. Tidal currents from a 30-day run are harmonically analysed (M2, S2, N2, K1, O1, with K2, P1 and Q1 inferred), so they can be predicted for any date with nodal corrections. They're shown here at 300 m resolution.
  • Wind-driven flow. The same two models are run to a steady state under a 7 m/s wind from each of 8 directions. Your wind is interpolated between those runs and scaled with speed. The "typical for the season" setting uses rough monsoon norms: NW winds about December–March, SE trades about May–October, lighter and variable in the changeovers. Put in a forecast wind (e.g. from Windy) for a better answer.
  • Surface layer. For boats and kayaks it adds a wind drift of 2.5% of wind speed, downwind. Divers should use the depth-averaged layer.

Checks so far

  • The tide reproduces the Mahé gauge constituents. Predicted high and low waters at Baie Ste Anne for 27 Nov – 3 Dec 2026 agree with an independent published Praslin tide table to within about 2–15 minutes and 0.1 m.
  • Modelled tidal currents are weak: 0.1–0.3 kn over most of the area, peaking at about 0.5 kn at headlands and in the Praslin–La Digue and Félicité gaps. This matches mooring evidence that tides aren't the main driver of currents on the plateau. A steady 14 kn wind typically drives 0.15–0.35 kn, up to about 0.5 kn off headlands, so on most days wind matters as much as the tide or more.

What it can't capture

  • Inertial and regional currents. Moorings on the Seychelles Plateau show that much of the real current variability is near-inertial oscillation (period about 6.6 days at this latitude, set off by wind changes) and slower regional flow. Neither can be predicted from tides and today's wind alone, so expect unexplained drift of 0.1–0.3 kn.
  • Bathymetry. The depths are GEBCO 2026 (about 460 m resolution), which is poor here. It shows false shoals north of Praslin (floored at 12 m), and reef edges, channels and pinnacles are not resolved. Near-shore depths assume a fringing-reef slope. Flow squeezed between rocks and around headlands will be stronger than shown.
  • Waves, swell-driven reef currents, rip currents and vertical shear aren't modelled.

How to make it better

The biggest single gain would come from current observations: GPS tracks from drifting SMBs or drogued drifters, logged with the time and the wind. With those, the model's friction and wind response can be calibrated. The next biggest is better depths, from nautical chart soundings or local surveys. A high-resolution circulation model of the plateau also exists: UCLouvain's SLIM model, built with the Blue Economy Research Institute at UniSey.
