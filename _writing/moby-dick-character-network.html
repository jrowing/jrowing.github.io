I made a graph of Moby-Dick.

The rules: chop the book into paragraphs, find every named character, and draw a line between any two who turn up in the same paragraph. Bigger blob, more mentions. Thicker line, more shared paragraphs. You can drag them about. Ahab is the big red one in the middle; he's always the big one in the middle.

Mentions by chapter

Most often in the same paragraph as

Ahab gets named 501 times. Nobody else gets past 260. His thickest lines go to the whale (64 paragraphs) and Starbuck (59), and if you're doing the book for an exam you could probably write the essay from those two numbers.

Ishmael gets named 19 times. In the whole book. He opens with "Call me Ishmael" and Melville took it as a suggestion. He's off in a corner talking to nobody, despite narrating every word.

Stubb, Starbuck and Flask are knotted together in the middle with the harpooneers hanging off them. Fedallah only really connects to Ahab, but he was hidden below decks until Chapter 48, so it's hard to blame him for not mixing.

Peleg and Bildad share 36 paragraphs, about half of everything either of them ever does. They haggle over Ishmael's pay, wave the ship off and that's them done.

Who turns up when

Same counts, laid out by chapter this time. Top 16 characters, Chapter 1 on the left.

Brighter cells mean more mentions in that chapter, relative to that character's busiest chapter. Hover a cell for the count.

Queequeg owns the opening. Ahab is mentioned from Chapter 16 and doesn't appear until 28, which is a long time to keep your readers waiting for the main character. The whale goes missing for long stretches in the middle while Ishmael explains blubber. The last three chapters, the chase, are the brightest bit of the whole chart, with Ahab on 24, 27 and 36 mentions. The Epilogue column is empty because almost everyone is dead.

How it was counted

Gutenberg text via Python's NLTK, split on chapter headings and then paragraphs. Names were matched with a few aliases ("the Parsee" for Fedallah, "White Whale" for Moby Dick, "the blacksmith" for Perth, "landlord" for Peter Coffin). Pronouns aren't counted, and neither is "the old man", so Ahab's real total is a lot higher.

A link is one paragraph that names both. It's crude. Jonah and Gabriel are mostly Bible references, Steelkilt and Radney only exist inside a story someone tells in Chapter 54, and it has no idea who is actually talking to whom.

The narrator of the entire novel comes out as a minor character. Ask a class why.
