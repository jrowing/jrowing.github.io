Spurious correlations: school science edition

Every number is real. Pick a school or science statistic and something unrelated, then watch them line up. Correlation is not causation.

This page is a homage to Tyler Vigen’s Spurious Correlations, the website and the 2015 book that made Nicolas Cage and swimming-pool drownings famous. The idea, the original US datasets and the “Vigen’s originals” pairs below are his work; the UK school-science series are my additions. If you like this, go and explore his site, which has tens of thousands more.

Each line is stretched to fill the chart’s height, the trick behind every spurious-correlation graph. Hover for real values.

    Questions for the classroom

    1. Find a pair with r above 0.9. Write one sentence a tabloid might print about it, then one sentence explaining why it’s wrong.
    2. Look at “r of year-on-year changes”. Why does it usually collapse when the main r is high? What is the hidden third variable?
    3. Pick a pair with only 6–8 overlapping years. How might the r value change if you had 50 years of data?
    4. There are hundreds of possible pairs here. If you test enough pairs, why are you guaranteed to find some strong correlations?
    5. Name a real correlation in physics where we do believe there’s a cause. What evidence beyond r makes us confident?
